This is a C++ port of a 1994 DOS game, reconstructed by decompiling the
original binary. The comments record where each fact came from in the original
machine code - addresses like `1000:86b8`, offsets like `DS:0x2056`, measured
counts and byte sizes. The Pascal source is lost, so these comments and the
binary are the only record of how the game works. Keep every fact, every
number and every address exactly as it is. Where the text is uncertain about
something, keep it uncertain.

The comments also shout: words are capitalised for emphasis several times per
file, and there is some stray markdown. That is the thing most worth fixing.
Beyond it, use your judgement - split what is tangled, cut what is padding,
straighten what is awkward. You do not need permission for a change that makes
the meaning easier to see.

Two things are not yours to edit. Code outside comments, including its
whitespace. And any block indented four or more spaces inside a comment: those
are quoted Pascal, assembly or tables lifted from the original program, and
they need to survive character for character, semicolons included.

Semicolons and dashes in the prose are fine. Do not hunt them.

Addresses, filenames and identifiers are written `in backticks` throughout this
codebase. Keep that.

Keep `//` comments wrapped at 79 columns.
